Meet Dr. Smiles

Dr. Smiles is Dr. John Vianney Sserumaga, a cosmetic dentist based in Bukoto, Kampala, focused on diagnosing oral disease early and helping people keep their teeth healthy for life.

The approach is simple: catch problems before they grow, explain every step in plain language, and keep good dental care within reach. That's why prevention and patient education sit at the heart of every visit, and why Dr. Smiles regularly takes dentistry out of the clinic and into communities through mobile services and charity projects.

Smile school

Pick a topic for four quick tips from Dr. Smiles. Each one takes less than a minute to read.

Keeping your teeth white

Tip 1 of 4

Rinse after your cuppa

Swish some water around after coffee, tea or cola.

Dark drinks leave colour that clings to your enamel. A quick rinse washes much of it away before it settles in.

Quit tobacco, keep the shine

Stopping smoking or chewing tobacco is one of the best things you can do for your smile.

Tar and nicotine stain teeth yellow and brown and raise the risk of gum disease. Once you stop, new stains stop building up.

Let us polish the tough stuff

Book a professional scale and polish at your regular check-up.

Some stains and tartar won't shift with a toothbrush. We remove them gently in the chair, and teeth often look brighter straight away.

Whiten safely

Talk to your dentist before trying any whitening product.

Lemon, baking soda and charcoal tricks can wear away enamel, and enamel doesn't grow back. A check-up first makes sure whitening is safe for your teeth and gums.

Everyday oral hygiene

Tip 1 of 4

Two minutes, twice a day

Brush every morning and every night for two minutes with a fluoride toothpaste.

Fluoride makes enamel stronger and helps stop decay. The night brush matters most, because your mouth makes less saliva while you sleep.

Spit, don't rinse

After brushing, spit out the toothpaste but skip rinsing with water.

Leaving a thin layer of toothpaste on your teeth gives the fluoride more time to protect them.

Clean between your teeth

Use floss or small interdental brushes once a day.

Your toothbrush can't reach the gaps between teeth, which is exactly where plaque hides and where decay and gum problems often start.

A fresh brush every 3 months

Replace your toothbrush every three months, or sooner if the bristles splay.

Worn bristles don't clean well. A soft or medium brush is kindest to your gums, and it's worth changing it after you've been ill too.

Foods and drinks that affect your teeth

Tip 1 of 4

Keep sweets to mealtimes

Enjoy sugary treats with a meal rather than between meals.

Every sugary snack starts an acid attack on your teeth that lasts for a while. Fewer attacks a day means less chance of decay.

Water is your best friend

Make plain water your everyday drink.

Soda, juice and energy drinks are packed with sugar and acid. Water rinses food away and keeps your mouth fresh.

Wait before you brush

After acidic food or drinks, wait about an hour before brushing.

Acid from soda, citrus and juice softens enamel for a while, and brushing right away can wear it down. Rinse with water in the meantime.

Snack smart

Choose nuts, cheese, plain yoghurt or crunchy vegetables as snacks.

They're low in sugar, and crunchy foods get saliva flowing, which is your mouth's natural defence. Sticky sweets cling to teeth for much longer.

Habits that damage your teeth

Tip 1 of 4

Teeth are not tools

Don't use your teeth to open bottles, tear packets or cut thread.

It's one of the most common ways teeth chip and crack. Keep an opener or scissors handy instead.

Don't crunch ice or chew pens

Avoid biting on ice, pens, pencils or fingernails.

Hard objects can crack enamel and damage fillings, and the habit strains your jaw over time.

Grinding at night?

If you wake up with a sore jaw or headaches, ask your dentist about grinding.

Many people clench or grind in their sleep without knowing it. A custom night guard can protect your teeth from wearing down.

Stop the all-day sip

Finish sweet drinks in one go instead of sipping them over hours.

Slow sipping keeps your teeth under constant attack from sugar and acid, with no break for saliva to repair them.

Healthy gums

Tip 1 of 4

Bleeding gums are a warning

Don't ignore gums that bleed when you brush or floss.

Bleeding is often the first sign of gingivitis, early gum disease. Caught early, it can usually be turned around with good cleaning and a dental visit.

Aim for the gumline

Tilt your brush towards the gums and use gentle, small circles.

Plaque collects where your teeth meet your gums. Gentle brushing there cleans it away; scrubbing hard can make gums pull back.

Tartar needs a professional

Have tartar removed with a regular scale and polish.

When plaque hardens into tartar, brushing can no longer remove it, and it keeps irritating your gums until it's cleaned off.

Healthy body, healthy gums

Tell your dentist about health conditions like diabetes, and about smoking.

Both raise the risk of gum disease, so your dentist may suggest more frequent check-ups to keep your gums in good shape.

Common dental mistakes

Tip 1 of 4

Brushing too hard

Use a soft brush and a light touch.

Scrubbing hard wears away enamel and pushes gums back. It's good technique, not force, that removes plaque.

Waiting for toothache

Don't wait for pain before you see a dentist.

Decay and gum disease often don't hurt in the early stages. Regular check-ups catch problems while they're small and simpler to treat.

Forgetting your tongue

Gently brush or scrape your tongue every day.

Bacteria on the tongue are one of the most common causes of bad breath.

Mouthwash instead of brushing

Use mouthwash as an extra, never as a replacement.

Mouthwash freshens your breath but doesn't remove plaque. Brushing and cleaning between your teeth do the real work.
